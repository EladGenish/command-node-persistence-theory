# Command-Node Persistence Theory

*A Perspectival Aspect Theory of Command, Maintenance, Orientation, Macro Formation, Fragmentation, and Collapse*

**Elad Genish**  
**Formal Edition — 14 July 2026**

> Everything that holds is holding against something.

## Abstract

Command-Node Persistence Theory (CNPT) is a general framework for analysing how objects persist under what bears on them. It begins from one base term, **object**, and treats node, command, structure, frame, world, orientation, utility, micro, and macro as aspect-positions rather than separate kinds of entity. An object is node from its own maintenance position and command when it bears survival-relevantly on another object. Command is carried as shape: either directly in the focal node's physiology or through a structure the node builds, finds, or uses. Maintenance holds the resulting command-shaped form, while function names what the object is maintaining itself as. Orientation is the object's total real-time position against its command topology from its own side; utility is the same functional relation read from another position.

The framework then explains how local persistence scales. Similar objects facing related command topologies can independently converge on, transmit, retain, and standardise survivable forms. A higher macro object forms when lower objects and relations jointly maintain a field-level function that has its own orientation and its own command-world. The higher object can return through structures that become command for the lower worlds maintaining it. CNPT uses this architecture to distinguish stability from hollow stability, to analyse recognition and power, and to define collapse and fragmentation without equating collapse with disappearance.

Its central claim is that persistence is not the absence of command. Persistence is the maintained carrying of command in shape.

> **Core relation:** Command hits node. The node bears the command by shape. Maintenance holds the command-shaped form. The command therefore persists in the object's present organisation rather than vanishing when contact ends.

## 1. Introduction

A body holds against hunger, injury, fatigue, temperature, and time. A school holds against disorder, inspection, funding limits, legitimacy demands, and the difficulty of coordinating people. A theory holds against contradiction, evidence, rival descriptions, and misuse. A state holds against pressures distributed across law, territory, money, population, force, infrastructure, and recognition.

CNPT is a theory of that holding: how an object persists under what bears on it, how the bearing becomes retained in form, how maintenance is distributed across levels, and what it means when the holding changes or fails.

CNPT does not begin by dividing reality into different ontological classes. It begins from object and asks how the same object appears from different relational positions. The object from its own maintenance position is node. The object as survival-relevant bearing-on another is command. The object as a held form is structure. The object as reader is frame. The object as encountered from a focal node is world. Orientation and utility are positional readings of function. Macro and micro are positions in a maintenance relation rather than measures of size.

The theory is dynamic, but its dynamics should not be reduced to a generic feedback loop. Command is not a signal that disappears after processing, and the node does not answer it and return to neutrality. Command is borne. The node's physiology takes the command-shape directly, or the node builds, finds, or uses structure that bears that shape. If the form continues, maintenance continues to carry the command. The past therefore survives in present organisation: in a scar, posture, routine, timetable, legal category, border, institutional habit, or conceptual distinction.

The same architecture scales. Similar objects facing related problem-spaces can converge on similar survivable forms. Forms can also be copied, required, borrowed, or standardised. When lower objects and relations jointly maintain a field-level function, the convergence becomes more than resemblance: it becomes a higher macro object with its own orientation. That object can then bear downward on the lower worlds that maintain it.

## 2. Object and Aspect

CNPT uses **object** as its primitive term. It does not ground objecthood in a privileged substrate such as matter, energy, consciousness, information, or language. An object is whatever can stand as a maintained unity within relations at the level being analysed. A rock, body, school, law, identity, theory, state, record, or routine may each be treated as object when its maintained unity is the focus of analysis.

The aspects of CNPT are not separate substances or mutually exclusive categories. They are positions that objects occupy in relations. The same object may occupy several aspects at once, and a change of focal position can change the aspect without multiplying or replacing the object.

### Node

The object from its own first-person maintenance position: the position from which its function is at stake.

Every object is node from its own side. Nodehood does not require consciousness, language, intention, or reflective awareness. A rock has nodehood because its held form can persist, shift, fracture, erode, or cease to hold. A school has nodehood because school-function can be maintained, reorganised, or lost. A theory has nodehood because its distinctions and explanatory function can hold, change, or collapse.

### Structure

The object as a maintained form that bears command: static from outside at the relevant level and maintained from inside.

Static does not mean absolutely motionless. It means that the object reads as one standing form at the level being analysed while lower maintenance worlds carry the movement that keeps it standing.

### Command

The object as survival-relevant bearing-on another object.

Command need not be spoken, intended, conscious, legal, personal, or externally imposed in the ordinary sense. Hunger, gravity, debt, pain, a deadline, recognition, misrecognition, a law, a timetable, a parent, a border, a market, an injury, or a contradiction can each occupy command-aspect.

Not every contact is command. Contact becomes command where it bears on what the focal node is maintaining itself as.

### Frame

The object as reader of another object from a third-person position.

A frame is not a neutral view from nowhere. It is another object reading from where it stands. A school frames a student. A state frames a citizen. A doctor frames a body. A theory frames a case. CNPT itself occupies frame-aspect when it analyses an object.

### World

The object as encountered from a focal node's survival position as part of a command-field.

World is not a neutral environment outside object. It is object as it bears on a node from the node's outward position.

### Orientation

The object's function read from its own side as the total real-time position of its physiology against command.

Orientation is not belief, mood, opinion, conscious intention, or a hidden programme. It is the object's resolved own-side position against the command topology currently bearing on it.

### Utility

The same functional relation read from another object's frame.

Utility is not more objective than orientation. It is third-person and positional. It tracks function, not substance.

### Micro and macro

Micro is the object as what maintains another object or field. Macro is the object as what is maintained by lower objects or relations.

Macro and micro do not mean large and small. They are local positions in a maintenance relation. An object can be macro in one relation and micro in another.

> **Aspect rule:** Objects do not belong to aspects. Objects occupy aspects. A change of aspect changes the position from which an object is read, not the substance of the object.

## 3. Physiology, Function, Maintenance, and Identity

### Physiology

Physiology is the node-side organisation through which an object takes shape, holds function, and meets command.

Physiology is not restricted to biology. A school's staffing, routines, records, material arrangements, authority relations, and habits can belong to school-physiology. A theory's distinctions, inferential dependencies, exclusions, examples, and methods can belong to theory-physiology.

Physiology is not an additional aspect. It names the inside organisation of the object from its node position.

### Function

Function is what an object maintains itself as at the level under analysis. It is the maintained doing or being that makes the object that object in the relevant relation.

Function is not substance, label, externally assigned purpose, or conscious intention. A building can remain while school-function disappears. A text can remain while theory-function disappears. A person can continue performing a role after the role-function has collapsed from their own side.

### Identity

Identity is judged by continuity of function at the relevant level.

This does not mean an object has only one function at every level. CNPT requires the analyst to specify the function and level under analysis. A body, person, citizen, employee, student, and family member may be overlapping functions held in different fields.

### Maintenance

Maintenance is the ongoing node-side holding of command-shaped physiology or command-shaped structure through which function continues.

Maintenance can be biological, social, symbolic, legal, cognitive, institutional, emotional, material, technical, conceptual, or practical. The kind of maintenance depends on the object and world being analysed.

Maintenance is usually not conscious. A person does not know every cellular process maintaining the body. A school-field does not know every student or teacher cost that holds it. A state does not know every household burden that keeps it functioning. A theory does not know every hidden assumption that lets it continue.

> **Core distinction:** Function names what continues. Maintenance names the holding through which it continues. Command names what bears survival-relevantly on that continuation. Shape names how that bearing is carried.

## 4. Encounter, Determination, Shape, and Time

### 4.1 Directional encounter

Consider two objects, A and B. Both are nodes from their own sides. Both have physiology. Their encounter can be read directionally.

From A's position, B occupies command-aspect where B bears survival-relevantly on A:

`B[command] -> A[node]`

From B's position, A may simultaneously occupy command-aspect:

`A[command] -> B[node]`

These are not contradictory accounts. They are the same encounter read from opposite maintenance positions.

Reciprocity does not imply symmetry. The two objects may command one another with radically unequal exposure, effect, and cost.

### 4.2 Relational determination

The shape produced in an encounter is not contained in either object alone. It is determined through the relation between the physiologies that meet.

A rock resting on ground is not the ground. Yet the ground is present in the rock's maintained position and shape. The rock's weight is likewise present in the compressed or displaced ground. Each object carries the other according to its own physiology.

CNPT distinguishes object-side determination from frame-side calculation. The rock and ground do not need to calculate. Their physiologies determine what their contact can force. A geologist, mathematical model, or other frame may calculate or derive that determination.

### 4.3 The unfinished whole and potential

Before a particular encounter is forced into a determinate command-shape, the physiologies already fix a space of possible shapes. CNPT calls the encounter under this indeterminacy the **unfinished whole**.

The unfinished whole is not simply the temporal stage called before. Before, during, and after belong to the actual bearing of a command. The unfinished whole is the relation read under unforced form wherever it stands in time.

Potential names shapes fixed by physiology but not yet forced by command. Potential does not enter orientation. Orientation is the object's resolved position under actual command; potential is derived by a frame from the physiologies involved.

### 4.4 Bearing by shape

When command hits node, the command must be borne through shape. There are two routes relative to the focal node.

**Direct physiological bearing:** the focal node's own physiology takes the command-shape. The command reshapes, erodes, trains, wounds, bends, or reorganises the node directly.

**Mediated structural bearing:** the focal node builds, finds, or uses a structure that bears the command-shape. A timetable, rule, record, distinction, routine, institution, border, role, treatment, or tool may bear command.

The routes can combine, but they should not be collapsed into one. A person can carry stress in bodily tension while also using a schedule to bear part of the same command. A school can reorganise staff culture while building new reporting systems.

### 4.5 Persistence of command

The command does not vanish when borne. It persists through the shape taken in physiology or structure.

A scar carries injury. A routine carries earlier disorder. A border carries conflict, movement, and classification command. A timetable carries coordination command. A theoretical distinction carries contradiction or explanatory pressure.

The event therefore gives before, during, and after:

- **Before:** the command-shape is not yet forced in this encounter.
- **During:** command bears on node and shape is being resolved.
- **After:** the command is now carried in changed physiology or maintained structure.

After does not mean that command is gone or automatically becomes the next command. It means that the bearing has become retained in present organisation.

A maintained shape may later enter another survival-relevant relation and occupy command-aspect there. A timetable built by a school becomes command to a teacher or student where it bears on their function. This requires no additional mechanism: the same object has entered another relation and changed aspect-position.

## 5. Orientation and Growth

Orientation is the object's total own-side position of physiology against the command topology currently bearing on it.

Orientation is not a processing stage placed between input and output. It is the resolved position from which the object holds, moves, changes, and maintains itself. There is no second coherence logic behind it.

If command changes, orientation changes. If physiology changes, orientation changes. A small change in command may produce a small shift in orientation; a severe or accumulated command may reorganise the object's whole direction.

Growth happens from orientation.

Growth is orientation moving into structure. It is not simply size, surplus, improvement, or expansion. When an object's resolved own-side position becomes retained in physiology or structure, the object has grown in the CNPT sense.

Growth can strengthen maintenance, damage lower worlds, thicken a macro field, produce hollow stability, or contribute to collapse. It is not automatically good.

## 6. Retention, Convergence, Standardisation, and Macro Formation

### 6.1 Similar problem-spaces

Similar objects often face overlapping command topologies. Schools educating comparable age groups face recurring problems of time, attendance, coordination, teaching, assessment, funding, legitimacy, and regulation. Their physiologies and local worlds are not identical, but their problem-spaces overlap.

Forms that maintain viability can be retained.

### 6.2 Parallel convergence and transmission

Similar forms can emerge through at least two routes.

**Parallel convergence:** different objects independently develop similar forms because they orient against similar command topologies.

**Transmission:** a form maintained successfully in one object is observed, copied, borrowed, required, taught, or otherwise taken up by another.

Standardisation is therefore not only imposed from above. It can emerge from similar objects solving similar problems, retaining what works, and converging around survivable forms.

The generative sequence is:

`related functions -> overlapping command topologies -> orientation -> survivable form -> retention -> convergence and/or transmission -> standardisation`

### 6.3 Resemblance is not macro formation

Similarity alone does not create a higher macro object. Ten schools can resemble one another without jointly maintaining a district, ministry, or educational field.

A macro object forms when lower objects and relations jointly maintain a field-level function that no one lower object maintains alone. Shared records, funding relations, legal recognition, inspection systems, qualification standards, administrative dependencies, common categories, and authority relations can jointly maintain a higher field.

A higher macro object therefore requires:

1. a field-level function;
2. lower objects and relations that maintain it;
3. a field-level physiology through which it holds;
4. orientation against commands at its own level;
5. capacity to enter relations as node, command, frame, world, structure, micro, and macro.

Macro formation is not a magical instant. It accretes through maintenance, recognition, dependency, retention, transmission, and standardisation.

### 6.4 Macro return

Once the higher object holds, it has the same aspect architecture as every object. An education system is node from its own side, structure as a maintained field, command when it bears on schools, and frame when it reads schools.

Its orientation concerns commands at its own level: legitimacy, national performance, funding, labour demand, political pressure, law, competition, and administration.

The macro object can produce structures—standards, targets, categories, inspections, funding rules, and reporting systems—that return as command topology for the lower worlds maintaining it.

The larger recursive movement is:

`local command -> local orientation -> retained form -> convergence/transmission -> macro accretion -> macro orientation -> macro structure -> lower command topology -> renewed local orientation`

This is not merely bottom-up emergence followed by top-down control. In CNPT terms, lower worlds maintain the macro object, while the macro object occupies command-aspect in relation to those lower worlds. Both remain nodes from their own sides.

## 7. Stability, Hollow Stability, Collapse, and Fragmentation

### Stability

Stability means the relevant command-shaped form and function still hold because maintenance still holds.

Stability does not mean no movement, no command, or no internal cost. From outside, structure appears static; from inside, maintenance continuously holds it.

### Hollow stability

Hollow stability means structure still appears held from outside while the maintenance inside is being spent, damaged, overloaded, displaced, reorganised, or broken.

A school still operates while teachers and students are being drained. A body still functions while compensation damages lower systems. A state appears orderly while households or excluded groups carry the cost. A theory still explains everything while refusing correction.

Hollow stability is divergence between the outside standing form and the inside maintenance of the same object.

### Collapse

Collapse is not object disappearance. Collapse occurs when maintenance can no longer hold the relevant function and command-shaped form at the macro level being analysed.

CNPT identifies three collapse dynamics.

#### Utility failure

The relevant constitutive frame no longer reads the function as viable, and that reading participated in maintaining the field-held function.

Not every observer is constitutive. A frame is constitutive only where its reading is part of what keeps the function standing at that level. A university's recognition may be constitutive of student-function in the university field; a stranger's opinion is not.

#### Physiology change

The object's maintenance reorganises around a different function.

The object may remain visible and retain its name while no longer maintaining the same function. A school that reorganises around metric production rather than education may remain institutionally open while school-function has collapsed by transformation.

Physiology taking command-shape is not automatically collapse. Ordinary bearing can change physiology while preserving function. Collapse by physiology change occurs when maintenance reorganises around another function.

#### Full maintenance failure

Maintenance no longer holds the relevant structure or function at that level. A body dies. A school closes or ceases to hold as school-field. A theory is no longer maintained as a living theory.

### Fragmentation

Macro collapse is absolute at the macro level where the constitutive maintenance relation fails. But lower maintenance worlds do not necessarily collapse with it.

Fragmentation is local continuation beneath an absolute macro collapse.

A school can collapse while teachers, students, routines, documents, friendships, buildings, methods, and habits continue and re-anchor elsewhere. A theory can collapse while distinctions, examples, and problems survive in another theory. A state can collapse while local institutions, infrastructures, identities, records, and practices remain.

Fragmentation is not partial macro collapse. It is the continuation of objects that were never only the macro field they helped maintain.

## 8. Recognition, Power, and Displaced Maintenance Cost

### Recognition

Recognition becomes command where it bears survival-relevantly on what an object maintains itself as.

Being named, denied, seen, included, excluded, classified, misread, praised, humiliated, legalised, or delegitimised can change structure, orientation, maintenance cost, utility reading, and identity.

Recognition may be constitutive of a field-held function. A student must be recognised as a student for student-function to hold in a school-field. A citizen must be recognised as a citizen for citizen-function to hold in a state-field.

Misrecognition is not merely an incorrect opinion. Where it bears on maintenance, it is command that forces the object to hold under a frame reading its function wrongly.

### Power

Power is asymmetric command under unequal reciprocal exposure.

An object or macro field has power where it structures another object's command-world while protecting its own maintenance from equivalent command in return.

A school structures time, grades, sanctions, progression, recognition, and legitimacy for students. A state structures law, money, borders, rights, records, policing, and recognition for citizens. A dominant frame can determine what function counts and what orientation is ignored.

### Displaced maintenance cost

A macro field can preserve its visible structure by pushing maintenance cost into lower worlds.

A school preserves metrics by displacing cost into teachers and students. A firm preserves profit by displacing cost into workers. A state preserves order by displacing cost into households, bodies, institutions, or excluded groups. A family preserves its image by making one member carry contradiction.

Extraction can describe particular cases, but it is not the primitive. The more general mechanism is displaced maintenance cost:

`higher structure holds -> maintenance burden is displaced -> lower world pays`

Where lower worlds continue holding under damaging cost, the macro may appear stable while becoming hollow.

## 9. Correction and Delusion

Correction is command capable of changing how a structure is maintained.

Correction is not merely information. It matters when evidence, pain, contradiction, failure, another frame, a lower maintenance world, the body, or social consequence bears survival-relevantly on the object.

A stable structure can take correction and preserve function through reorganisation. A hollow structure often treats correction as danger because correction would expose failing maintenance or changed physiology underneath.

Delusion is hollow stability in a cognitive frame. A delusional structure may hold, feel clear, reduce anxiety, and explain everything, but it does so by preventing correction from reshaping it. Contradiction becomes proof, denial becomes confirmation, and absence becomes hidden presence.

The same mechanism can occur in a theory, ideology, institution, family, state, or person.

CNPT must apply this account to itself. If CNPT reorganises around protection from criticism rather than clarification, it has changed physiology and become hollow by its own terms.

## 10. Formal Schema

Let `Obj` be the base type.

For any object `O`, `O[a]` means object `O` read under aspect `a`, where:

`a in {node, command, structure, frame, world, orientation, utility, micro, macro}`

The auxiliary terms `physiology`, `maintenance`, and `function` name the object from its own inside and do not enlarge the aspect set.

### Axiom 1: Universal nodehood

For every object `O`, `O[node]` exists from O's own maintenance position.

### Axiom 2: Aspect relativity

Aspect-position depends on the relation and focal position; it does not create or replace the object.

### Axiom 3: Command relation

`Command(O,P,F,L)` holds when O bears survival-relevantly on P's maintained function F at level L.

### Axiom 4: Directionality

`Command(O,P,F,L)` does not imply `Command(P,O,F,L)` and does not imply equal effect where the reverse relation also holds.

### Axiom 5: Relational determination

The command-shape carried by P is determined through the encounter between O's bearing and P's physiology.

### Axiom 6: Two routes of bearing

P bears command through either direct physiological shape, mediated structure, or a combination of the two.

### Axiom 7: Command persistence

A borne command persists as maintained shape in P's physiology or structure.

### Axiom 8: Functional identity

Identity at level L is judged by continuity of the specified maintained function at L.

### Axiom 9: Maintenance relativity

Where A maintains B, A is micro relative to B and B is macro relative to A.

### Axiom 10: Macro accretion

A higher macro object holds where lower objects and relations jointly maintain a field-level function with field-level physiology, orientation, and relational capacity.

### Axiom 11: Level-specific collapse

Collapse occurs where the constitutive maintenance of the specified function fails at the level under analysis; lower objects may continue from their own node positions.

### Derived propositions

**Retained-history proposition:** If command persists as maintained shape, present organisation can carry the history of earlier bearing without the original contact remaining present.

**Asymmetric-reciprocity proposition:** Two objects may command one another in one encounter while carrying radically unequal effects and maintenance costs.

**Convergence proposition:** Similar objects under related command topologies can generate similar forms without direct copying or top-down imposition.

**Macro-return proposition:** A macro object maintained by lower worlds can produce structures that become command topology for those same lower worlds.

**Hollow-stability proposition:** Visible structural continuity does not entail intact maintenance.

**Fragmentation proposition:** Absolute macro collapse is compatible with local continuation of lower maintenance worlds.

## 11. Worked Examples

### Rock and ground

Rock and ground are distinct objects. From the rock's side, ground occupies command-aspect where support, angle, pressure, friction, or instability bears on the rock's held form. The rock's orientation is its physiology resolved against this topology. Its resting position, deformation, fracture, or movement carries ground-shape.

From the ground's side, the rock can occupy command-aspect. Its weight and contact alter compression, displacement, or fracture in the ground. The encounter is reciprocal but need not be symmetric.

The ground is not inside the rock, but its bearing persists in the rock's position and shape. The rock is not the ground, but the ground has an imprint in what the rock is able to maintain.

### Body

A body faces hunger, fatigue, illness, injury, desire, shame, temperature, pain, threat, and rest. It bears command directly through scar, muscle, posture, stress-pattern, habit, hormonal change, pain-pattern, and adaptation. It can also use structure: treatment, shelter, routine, tool, role, or schedule.

The body is macro relative to organs and cells that maintain it, and micro relative to social worlds it helps maintain. Death is full maintenance failure at the body macro level. Lower tissues, traces, memories, records, and social effects may continue as fragments.

### School and education system

A school is node from its own side and command to students and teachers where it structures time, attendance, recognition, assessment, and progression. Inspection, funding, legitimacy, regulation, coordination, and educational demand become command to the school.

The school bears them through physiology—staffing patterns, workload, culture, defensive routines—and structure—timetables, classrooms, grades, policies, records, and sanctions.

Similar schools facing related problem-spaces can independently converge on or copy survivable forms. Where shared relations jointly maintain a district, ministry, or educational field, a higher macro object forms. Its standards, targets, inspections, and funding rules return as command topology to schools.

The field may look coherent while lower worlds carry excessive cost. Schools reorganise, teachers absorb workload, students absorb stress, and bodies absorb exhaustion. The macro can therefore become hollowly stable while remaining visibly operational.

### Theory

A theory is node from its own side and structure as a maintained distinction-system. Evidence, contradiction, rival explanations, examples, and criticism become command where they bear on theory-function.

A stable theory can take correction while preserving clarification. A hollow theory protects outward coherence by preventing correction from reaching maintenance. If it reorganises around self-protection rather than clarification, it collapses by physiology change. Its distinctions and examples may survive as fragments elsewhere.

### Identity

Identity is a maintained function rather than substance or label alone. From the person's own side, identity has orientation. Social, legal, institutional, and interpersonal frames read utility and recognition.

Recognition can support a field-held identity-function. Misrecognition can become command that increases maintenance cost or forces additional structure. A frame is constitutive only where its reading participates in maintaining the identity-function at the level being analysed.

Social identity, legal identity, role identity, and self-maintained identity may therefore differ without being collapsed into one.

## 12. CNPT as Its Own Object

CNPT is itself an object.

It is node from its own side because theory-function is at stake in its distinctions. It is structure as this formed account. It is command when it bears on another theory, reader, institution, or problem. It is frame when it reads a case. It is world when another object encounters it. It is macro relative to the concepts and examples maintaining it and micro relative to any field it helps sustain.

CNPT can grow, become hollow, change physiology, fragment, or collapse. Its anti-corruption rules are therefore maintenance rules for the theory's own function.

## 13. Anti-Corruption Rules

1. Do not treat node, command, structure, frame, world, orientation, utility, micro, and macro as separate substances. They are aspect-positions of objects.
2. Do not treat nodehood as optional or as a synonym for consciousness.
3. Do not treat every contact as command. Command is survival-relevant bearing-on a specified function at a specified level.
4. Do not erase direction. A command relation from A to B is distinct from one from B to A.
5. Do not infer symmetry from reciprocity.
6. Do not put a calculator inside the object. Object-side physiology determines shape; explicit calculation is frame work.
7. Do not treat command as discharged or removed by response. Command persists through maintained shape.
8. Do not add a universal answer step. Human answers are one possible form of bearing.
9. Do not collapse direct physiological bearing into structure-making.
10. Do not drop find and use. A node can recruit existing structure.
11. Do not treat structure as merely process. Structure is standing form from outside and maintenance from inside.
12. Do not treat physiology as biological substance only.
13. Do not place potential inside orientation.
14. Do not put a hidden coherence programme behind orientation.
15. Do not treat orientation and utility as separate parts added together.
16. Do not make utility context-free.
17. Do not let any observer count as constitutive.
18. Do not treat identity as matter, label, or appearance alone.
19. Do not turn macro and micro into fixed sizes or a universal ladder.
20. Do not treat resemblance as macro formation.
21. Do not switch macro layers on at a single magical threshold.
22. Do not make top-down imposition the only source of standardisation.
23. Do not make growth synonymous with surplus, size, or improvement.
24. Do not treat visible continuity as proof of stability.
25. Do not make extraction the primitive; the more general relation is displaced maintenance cost.
26. Do not treat collapse as disappearance.
27. Do not treat fragmentation as partial macro collapse.
28. Do not let CNPT protect itself from correction.

## 14. Concise Statement and Conclusion

Every object can occupy every CNPT aspect: node, command, structure, frame, world, orientation, utility, micro, and macro. These are positions in relations, not separate kinds of entity. Every object is node from its own side. Physiology names the node-side organisation through which it holds function and takes shape.

Command is survival-relevant bearing-on. When an object bears on a focal node's maintained function, it occupies command-aspect. The command is resolved through the node's physiology into a command-shape. The node carries that shape directly in physiology or through structure it builds, finds, or uses. Maintenance keeps the command-shaped form holding. The command therefore persists in present organisation.

Orientation is the object's total real-time position against its command topology from its own side. Utility is the same function read from another frame. Macro and micro are generated by maintenance: what maintains is micro, and what is maintained is macro, relative to the specified relation.

Maintained shape changes what later commands meet. Similar objects under related command topologies can retain, transmit, and converge on survivable forms. When lower objects and relations jointly maintain a field-level function that no single lower object can maintain alone, a macro object accretes. It has its own orientation and can return through structures that become command to the lower worlds maintaining it.

Stability means maintenance still holds the relevant function and command-shaped form. Hollow stability means the standing form remains while the maintenance underneath is being spent, damaged, displaced, or reorganised. Collapse occurs through constitutive utility failure, physiology change around another function, or full maintenance failure. Macro collapse is absolute at its level; fragmentation is the continuation of lower worlds beneath it.

CNPT is a theory of persistence as maintained command-shape. It explains how one object can remain itself while carrying the imprint of another, how present form can contain the history of earlier bearing, and how local solutions can become standards and then higher objects. It also explains why outward continuity can conceal internal exhaustion and why the collapse of a macro object does not require the disappearance of everything that maintained it.

The framework's smallest relation is directional: command hits node. Its central mechanism is shape. Its criterion of identity is function. Its engine of persistence is maintenance. Its own-side position is orientation. Its scale relation is macro and micro. Its generative movement is retention, convergence, macro accretion, and macro return. Its failure modes are hollow stability, transformation, utility failure, full maintenance failure, and fragmentation.

Everything that holds is holding against something. CNPT names the positions, forms, costs, and levels through which that holding becomes a world.
